Annual evidence, not commute exposure

The exported dataset contains 29 annual site-years from 4 physical stations in Durham and Mecklenburg counties, 2018–2025. These are measured EPA annual PM2.5 summaries. The curves are unweighted means across available stations, not population exposure or school measurements. Changes in station coverage can affect year-to-year differences.

Scope: annual EPA reference observations only. This report does not find or rule out traffic-related spikes. It documents the available evidence and the requirements for testing that question. The filename denotes the intended research question, not completed school-corridor validation. 2025 summaries are provisional.

What a corridor study must establish

Commute versus off-peak PM2.5, AQI spikes, school exposure, and the subset within 1 km of I-40 / I-85 are NOT ESTIMABLE from this export. Annual periods contain no hour-of-day observations. No road-distance or school join was performed. Missing meteorology remains null. An annual PM2.5 value must not be presented as an hourly AQI value.

Required next study: acquire quality-controlled hourly observations and weather; join verified highway centerlines using geodesic or projected point-to-line distance; identify school locations separately; define a 1,000 m buffer before examining outcomes. Pre-register weekday 07:00–09:00 and 16:00–19:00 America/New_York commute windows, matched off-peak hours, daylight-saving handling, completeness thresholds, and day-level uncertainty. Use an appropriate EPA AQI averaging protocol rather than applying breakpoints to annual averages. Spatial interpolation requires independent held-out stations and cannot create missing temporal evidence.
